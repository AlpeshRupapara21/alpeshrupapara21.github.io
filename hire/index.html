How I work

No surprises, in either direction

You will always know what is being built, what it costs and where it stands. I would rather lose a project at the scoping stage than deliver one that disappoints at the end.

Step 01

Scope call

A free 30-minute call. What you want, who it is for, what already exists, and what your real deadline is.

Step 02

Written proposal

Scope, milestones, timeline and a fixed price — in writing, before any money moves. If I think your idea needs cutting down, I say so here.

Step 03

Build in increments

Weekly builds you can actually install and use. You see progress continuously, not at the end.

Step 04

QA & security pass

Every change goes through a testing and security review gate before release — the same pipeline I run for my team.

Step 05

Release & handover

Code signing, store metadata, submission and review response. You get the repository, the documentation and a working pipeline — not a dependency on me.

Before you ask

Straight answers

Can you take over an existing codebase?

Yes — a large share of my work is inheriting someone else's project. I start with an audit and tell you honestly whether it should be continued or rebuilt, before you spend money either way.

Do you handle App Store submission?

End to end. Certificates, provisioning profiles, code signing, metadata, screenshots, TestFlight and the review response if it gets rejected. This is routine for me, not a risk.

Will you sign an NDA?

Yes, before any details are shared. I currently work under confidentiality across multiple products and take it seriously — you will notice this site describes architecture, never anyone's business strategy.

What are your working hours?

I am in India (IST, UTC+5:30), with comfortable overlap for European clients and early-morning overlap for US East Coast. I keep a fixed weekly call regardless of timezone.

Do you work with designers?

Happily — Figma hand-off is normal for me. If you do not have a designer I can build to a clean, platform-native standard, but I will tell you where a real designer would do better.

What if the project goes wrong?

Milestones exist so you can stop at any point and keep everything delivered so far. You own the code from the first commit, and it lives in your repository, not mine.

Send a couple of paragraphs about the project and your timeline. I reply to every serious enquiry within 24 hours — including the ones I turn down.
